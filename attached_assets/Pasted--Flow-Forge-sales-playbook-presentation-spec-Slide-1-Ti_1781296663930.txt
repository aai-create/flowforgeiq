# Flow Forge sales playbook — presentation spec

## Slide 1 — Title / positioning
**Title:** Flow Forge  
**Subtitle:** A supply chain communication platform for managing supplier, buyer, and shipment workflows

**Content:**
- Built for sourcing agencies, manufacturers, and trading teams managing many active POs, shipments, and supplier conversations
- Centralizes fragmented communication and workflow tracking across email and messaging channels
- Designed to reduce manual follow-up, missed updates, and operational drag

**Speaker note:**
Open with the category clearly. Do not pitch this as “just an AI app.” Pitch it as an operational platform for supply chain communication.

---

## Slide 2 — The problem
**Title:** Supply chain operations still run on fragmented communication

**Content:**
- Teams are chasing suppliers across email, WhatsApp, WeChat, and manual follow-ups
- Shipment status, payment tracking, and PO coordination often live in people’s heads or scattered tools
- Manual tracking creates delays, surprises, and avoidable mistakes
- Smaller and mid-sized operators often do not have a purpose-built system for this workflow

**Speaker note:**
Make the pain operational and concrete: supplier chasing, shipment surprises, and time lost to coordination.

---

## Slide 3 — Why now
**Title:** Why teams need this now

**Content:**
- Global supply chains are more volatile than ever
- Tariffs, geopolitical uncertainty, oil/shipping disruption, and sudden policy shifts create more exceptions to manage
- When uncertainty rises, communication gaps become more expensive
- Teams need faster visibility, cleaner coordination, and fewer manual handoffs

**Speaker note:**
This slide creates urgency. The point is not just “efficiency.” It is resilience in a volatile operating environment.

---

## Slide 4 — Origin story
**Title:** We built Flow Forge for ourselves first

**Content:**
- The concept came from real pain inside Sourceaccess’s own workflow
- The team wanted a better way to manage supplier communication, tracking, and follow-up without constant manual effort
- Instead of building software for the sake of it, the team built the tool they wished they already had
- That story becomes the foundation of the sales narrative

**Speaker note:**
This is a trust slide. It says: we know this workflow because we lived it.

---

## Slide 5 — What Flow Forge is
**Title:** What Flow Forge does

**Content:**
- A supply chain communication platform that helps teams manage workflows across suppliers, manufacturers, buyers, and shipments
- Centralizes communication inputs and turns them into actionable workflow context
- Helps teams handle multiple POs, SKUs, projects, and moving parts at the same time
- Best fit for operators with meaningful volume and coordination complexity

**Speaker note:**
Keep this operational. Avoid overly technical language.

---

## Slide 6 — Who it is for
**Title:** Ideal customer profile

**Content:**
- Sourcing agencies with many concurrent customer and supplier workflows
- Manufacturers managing many suppliers and buyers
- Teams with roughly 20–50+ active POs, SKUs, or projects where coordination overhead becomes material
- Best early adopters are businesses without heavy in-house software teams

**Speaker note:**
Be specific. This helps the audience qualify themselves quickly.

---

## Slide 7 — How it works
**Title:** How Flow Forge fits into the workflow

**Content:**
- Teams forward relevant messages and updates into the system through a companion app workflow
- The platform identifies the relevant context and routes the information into the correct operating instance
- PO-related information and communication history become easier to track and act on
- The goal is to reduce fragmented coordination, not force a full rip-and-replace of existing systems

**Speaker note:**
Important: describe the workflow accurately. Do not imply full passive monitoring.

---

## Slide 8 — Messaging and integrations
**Title:** Built for real-world communication stacks

**Content:**
- Supports workflows that begin in tools like WhatsApp and WeChat through manual forwarding into the companion app
- Automatic monitoring of those chat apps is not the model; those ecosystems are walled gardens
- Integrations with systems like SAP or Oracle are possible as custom-scoped projects
- The value proposition is flexibility without requiring a full system overhaul

**Speaker note:**
This slide reduces buyer anxiety. It answers “will this work with how we already operate?”

---

## Slide 9 — ROI
**Title:** The ROI is operational

**Content:**
- Faster response times across supplier and buyer communication
- Fewer shipment surprises and less manual coordination overhead
- Less time spent tracking details manually
- Example directional metrics discussed:
  - 50% faster response times
  - 30% fewer shipment surprises
  - 80% reduction in manual tracking

**Speaker note:**
Frame these as directional early metrics or placeholders until pilot data validates them. Do not overstate precision yet.

---

## Slide 10 — Before vs. after
**Title:** Before Flow Forge / After Flow Forge

**Content:**
- **Before:** scattered messages, manual tracking, reactive follow-up, limited visibility
- **After:** centralized workflow context, cleaner communication capture, faster action, better tracking discipline
- **Before:** people remember the workflow
- **After:** the process is more visible and structured

**Speaker note:**
Keep it simple and visual. This should be an easy skim slide.

---

## Slide 11 — Competitive landscape
**Title:** Why Flow Forge vs. enterprise systems or custom builds

**Content:**
- SAP, Oracle, and Salesforce can support pieces of this workflow, but are often heavy, generalized, or require custom implementation
- Custom internal tools require engineering bandwidth and ongoing maintenance
- Flow Forge is positioned as a focused, operationally simple layer for this specific problem
- Especially attractive for teams that need something usable without building from scratch

**Speaker note:**
This is not about claiming nobody else exists. It is about explaining the wedge.

---

## Slide 12 — Pilot structure
**Title:** Pilot program

**Content:**
- Initial pilot term: 3 months free
- In exchange, pilot partners commit to monthly feedback calls
- Goal is to validate workflow fit, ROI, and product improvements with real users
- Pilot starts small rather than moving an entire business onto the platform immediately

**Speaker note:**
This lowers risk. Make the pilot sound disciplined and collaborative.

---

## Slide 13 — Pricing
**Title:** Founding member pricing

**Content:**
- Founding offer discussed: $99/month or $1,000/year
- Framed as locked-in founding pricing
- Broader pricing likely evolves by company size and complexity
- Custom integrations and enterprise requirements priced separately

**Speaker note:**
Keep the pricing story simple. Founder pricing helps create urgency without overcomplicating packaging.

---

## Slide 14 — Proof / case study placeholder
**Title:** What pilot proof will look like

**Content:**
- Anonymized case study format for sourcing agencies and manufacturers
- Show workflow baseline, changes introduced, and outcomes observed
- This becomes the proof layer that supports future ROI claims
- Use one-slide snapshots that are specific but not identity-revealing

**Speaker note:**
If no proof exists yet, make this a placeholder or “what we are measuring in pilots” slide.

---

## Slide 15 — Product roadmap
**Title:** Expansion opportunities over time

**Content:**
- Supplier directory / marketplace where suppliers can be discovered and receive RFQs
- Localization path includes Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, and Korean
- Future industry expansion beyond manufacturing and sourcing is possible once initial fit is proven
- Financing / payment orchestration ideas remain future-phase, not core launch scope

**Speaker note:**
Keep roadmap focused. Show upside without muddying the current product story.

---

## Slide 16 — Call to action
**Title:** Pilot with us

**Content:**
- Looking for a small number of founding pilot partners
- Best fit: teams with real workflow complexity and openness to monthly feedback
- Start with a contained workflow, prove value, then expand

**Speaker note:**
End with a low-risk, concrete next step.
