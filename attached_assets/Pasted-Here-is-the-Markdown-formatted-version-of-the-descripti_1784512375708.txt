# Workbook Structure by Tracing Formula Logic Across Sheets

## Assumptions Made
* **Formula Evaluation:** Because the data is presented as values, it is assumed that standard spreadsheet formulas (such as `=SUM()`, product price multiplications, and cross-reference links) are driving the totals, grand totals, and commission rows.
* **Template Standardization:** The identical row indexes and labels across multiple sheets indicate a strict master template architecture used to maintain consistency across different supply categories.

---

## Key Takeaways of the Workbook Structure

### 1. Standardized Matrix-Based Architecture
All sheets in the workbook utilize a highly structured, column-oriented matrix design where columns represent individual **Purchase Orders (POs) or Shipping Batches**, and rows represent **Product Items and Metadata**.

* **Metadata Header Block (Rows 0–8):** Every sheet dedicates the first 9 rows to column-wise shipment metadata. This tracks critical logistical variables including the unique `PO#`, timeline dates (`Exfactory`, `ETD`, `PO Date`, or `Required ETA`), shipping mode (`VIA` e.g., Ocean, Air, LCL), and the `Destination` port or warehouse.
* **Top-Positioned Dashboard Summary (Row 9):** A merged `GRAND TOTAL` row is strategically placed directly beneath the headers. In a live sheet, this row acts as a dashboard link that references the financial totals calculated at the very bottom of each column, allowing users to see shipment values instantly without scrolling down hundreds of rows.
* **Granular Product Rows (Rows 20+):** Specific product variants are listed row-by-row (e.g., bag sizes, hanger styles, or light box dimensions) with their respective units of measure in Column 1, serving as the raw quantity inputs for cost formulas.

### 2. Two-Dimensional Formula Flow (Vertical & Horizontal Aggregations)
The formula logic in this workbook flows in two directions to provide both per-shipment and per-item visibility.

* **Horizontal Item Totals:** Trailing columns on the far right of the product rows utilize horizontal `=SUM()` formulas to aggregate the total volume shipped for each individual item across all historical POs.
* **Vertical Shipment Costs:** Columns calculate individual shipment values vertically. Shipped quantities are multiplied by their respective item unit prices in the `TOTAL USD AMOUNT` rows. These itemized costs are then summed into a `GRAND TOTAL USD AMOUNT` at the base of each column.
* **Secondary Financial Metrics:** Packaging sheets (such as *F21 Shopping Bags* and *Schwarz Shopping Bags*) extend this vertical logic to calculate logistics-specific metrics like `Total boxes order` and a `COMMISION TOTAL (3%)` derived directly from the column's grand total.

### 3. Chronological Continuity and Template Replication
The sheets are organized by product category and time period, establishing clear relationships between tabs.

* **Chronological Tab Splitting:** The *Hangers* and *Shopping Bags* categories demonstrate a parent-child relationship where data is split across tabs by year (e.g., `2009–2018` and `2019–2020`). Because they maintain identical row labels and structures, they are designed to support multi-tab consolidation or 3D formulas (e.g., `=SUM('Hangers:Hangers_2018-2021'!Column_Total)`).
* **Template Twins:** Tabs like *Ecommerce* and *Track_light_LED* share identical row structures and dimensions, confirming that a master template is cloned when tracking new supply categories to ensure workbook integrity.
